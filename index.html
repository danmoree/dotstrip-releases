Widgets

Four things worth a glance, and a dog.

Turn on as many as you like. They share the board side by side, each in its own zone, and a playing track takes the wide lane from the news until the music stops. Or switch them all off and hand the whole board to a graphic.

Now Playing

The track and artist from Spotify or Apple Music, with a thin progress bar riding along the top row.

News ticker

Headlines from any RSS or Atom feed you point it at, crawling past like a newsroom sign.

Stocks

Quotes for the symbols you track, with a little arrow for which way the day is going.

Weather

The temperature in a fixed zone that flips between now, today's high and today's low, with an animated sun and rain. Pick your city from a search so you get the right Springfield.

Graphics mode

Hand the whole panel to an animation instead. This dog trots, sniffs around, sits and wags, and now and then curls up to sleep. There are more to pick from in the app.

Made like hardware

It scrolls like a sign, not like text.

Most "tickers" are smooth text sliding over a background. DotStrip is built the way a physical board works.

Whole-dot scrollingText steps one dot-column at a time. Nothing is antialiased into place, so it reads as a lattice of lights.
A hand-drawn fontEvery letter is a 5×7 bitmap drawn dot by dot, the kind real signs use, instead of a font rasterized and hoped for the best.
Light on your MacThe unlit lattice is drawn once and only the lit dots move, so a board that never stops scrolling stays quiet in Activity Monitor.
Settings

Everything lives in one menu.

Click the panel and you get the lot. No preferences window, no dock icon. The grey rows at the top are a plain-text read-out of whatever has already scrolled past.

  • Show what you want. Switch each widget on or off, pick any RSS feed, edit your stock symbols.
  • Layout. Give each widget its own zone, or let them share one band and take turns.
  • Five colors. Amber, Green, Red, Ice and White, each with its own panel tint, as on real signs.
  • HDR Glow. On macOS 26 with a capable display, the lit dots push brighter than white.
  • Scroll speed to taste.

FAQ

Questions.

What do I need?

A Mac running macOS 13 Ventura or later. There is no Dock icon and no window: DotStrip is just the board in your menu bar, and clicking it opens the settings.

What happens after the 7 days?

The board stops until you subscribe. You'll get a prompt where you can buy or enter a license key you already have.

What permissions does it want?

Only Automation, the first time Now Playing looks for Spotify or Music. If you say no, that widget stays empty and nothing else is affected.

Where does the data come from?

Weather from Open-Meteo, stock quotes from Yahoo Finance, headlines from the feeds you choose. None of them needs an account or API key, and DotStrip keeps no analytics on you. See the privacy policy.

Will it burn my battery?

No. It is designed to redraw as little as possible, and you can pick a slower scroll speed from the menu.

Is it on the Mac App Store?

No, it's a direct download so updates can ship the moment they're ready. It's still signed and notarized, so it opens without a Gatekeeper warning.
